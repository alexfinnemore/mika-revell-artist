## INSTITUTION ANALYSIS

### Green Valley Inc.

**Mission:** "Make Rural Japan Cool!" - solving regional problems through arts and culture, building self-sustaining communities.

**Founded:** 2004 (predecessor Kamiyama International Exchange Association founded 1992)

**Three Visions:**
1. Develop creative rural areas where people are the main content
2. Develop "The World's Kamiyama" by integrating diverse wisdom
3. Develop sustainable community through "creative depopulation"

**Philosophy:** Named after Silicon Valley - while Kamiyama doesn't have silicon, it has "green" (rich natural environment). Both share an atmosphere of creating change.

**Approach:** Started KAIR in 1999 as seeds of an "open door policy" - attracting artists, then making it easier for companies to relocate. Kamiyama is now considered a "sacred place" for regional revitalization in Japan.

### KAIR Program

- **Running since:** 1999 (27 years)
- **Format:** 2-3 artists annually, Sept-Nov
- **Philosophy:** "Encounters with the unknown" - impact on both artists and community
- **Name meaning:** Kamiyama = "God's Mountain"
- **Focus:** Artists influenced by "timeless nature" and "friendly local people"

**Sources:** [Green Valley About](https://www.in-kamiyama.jp/en/about-us/), [Kamiyama Creative Depopulation](https://field-journal.com/issue-8/kamiyamas-success-in-creative-depopulation/)

## SITE & CONTEXT RESEARCH

### Geography

- **Location:** Tokushima Prefecture, Shikoku Island, Japan
- **Distance:** ~1 hour by car from Tokushima city
- **Terrain:** Mountainous, along the Akui River
- **River:** Famous for clear waters and blue-tinged stones (used by Isamu Noguchi for UNESCO sculpture)
- **Forest:** Evergreen woodland - hinoki (Japanese cypress) and sugi (Japanese cedar)

### Oawayama Art Mountain

- Protected and sacred forest
- Outdoor exhibition venue for KAIR
- Site of "In-Between" pavilion by x-studio Ivan Juarez
  - Built using traditional Japanese techniques
  - Used local hinoki and sugi wood
  - Community participation in construction
  - Part of Kamiyama's Forestry Management program

### Historical/Cultural Significance

- Mentioned in Nihon Shoki (Japan's earliest written records)
- Before 1990s: Primarily known for Buddhist and Shinto pilgrimage
- **Shōsan-ji:** Temple No. 12 on the Shikoku 88-temple pilgrimage (Henro)
- **Kaiichinomiya Shinto Shrine:** Features shimenawa sacred ropes on trees believed to house kami (spirits)

### Climate

- Program runs Sept-Nov (autumn)
- Mountainous microclimate
- Forested environment

**Sources:** [Japan Adventurer Kamiyama](https://japanadventurer.com/points-of-interest/tokushima/kamiyama/), [In-Between Pavilion](https://www.archdaily.com/888676/in-between-hinoki-plus-sugi-pavilion-x-studio)

## LOCAL CRAFT TRADITIONS

### Awa Indigo (藍染 Aizome)

Tokushima's signature craft - designated **Japan Heritage**

**History:**
- Records from 900s: "Indigo from Awa Province is the finest"
- Developed due to fertile Yoshino River basin
- Encouraged by Hachisuka clan (first lords of Tokushima domain)
- Known worldwide as "Japan Blue"

**Process:**
- Sukumo: natural dye from fermented indigo leaves
- 100+ days fermentation under rice straw mats
- Traditional Awa Shoai Shijira-ori weaving designated national traditional craft

**Color vocabulary:**
- Kamenozoki (瓶覗) - "peeping at a tub" - palest blue
- Hanadairo - light blue
- Aiiro - indigo blue
- Kachiiro - "winning color" - worn by samurai

**Potential connection:** Natural dyeing process, local materials, traditional techniques

### Shimenawa (Sacred Ropes)

**Definition:** Rice straw or hemp ropes marking sacred spaces in Shinto

**Symbolism:**
- Twisted rope = clouds (line between heaven and earth)
- Shide (zigzag paper) = thunder (kami's descent)
- Suzu (bells) = lightning (purification)

**Materials:** Traditionally hemp; now often rice straw (since 1948 Cannabis Control Act)

**Local presence:** Kaiichinomiya Shrine in Kamiyama has shimenawa on sacred trees

**Potential connection:** Rope-based installation, sacred/profane boundaries, natural materials

### Other Tokushima Crafts

- **Awa washi:** Traditional paper, uniquely thick and large (up to 4m sheets)
- **Yusanbako:** Lacquerware picnic boxes, Edo period tradition
- **Woodwork:** Hinoki and sugi carpentry traditions

**Sources:** [Awa Indigo](https://discovertokushima.net/en/crafts/awa-indigo-dyed/), [Shimenawa](https://www.japan-experience.com/plan-your-trip/to-know/understanding-japan/shimenawa)

## STRATEGIC INSIGHTS

### What KAIR Values

1. **Community integration** - Not just making art, but living with locals
2. **Process over product** - 69-day residency emphasizes journey
3. **Site-responsiveness** - Work influenced by Kamiyama specifically
4. **Cultural exchange** - Workshops, food exchange, open studios
5. **Working within constraints** - Limited resources, self-reliance valued

### Success Patterns from Past Artists

- Titles that evoke place, nature, liminality
- Work that engages with local materials/environment
- Collaborative/community involvement in making
- Conceptual depth with physical presence
- Outdoor installations on Oawayama particularly memorable

### Living Matter Alignment

| KAIR Value | Living Matter Strength |
|------------|----------------------|
| Nature connection | Biomimicry, natural materials |
| Community engagement | Workshop facilitation, collaborative process |
| Site-responsiveness | Deep research, local materials |
| Limited resources | Resourceful, sustainable practice |
| Cultural exchange | Mika's 2016 Japan residency, cross-cultural work |

### Differentiators for 2026

**Mika's advantages:**
- Prior Japan residency (AIR 3331 Arts Chiyoda, 2016)
- SFER IK Art in Nature Award winner (2024)
- Science-art intersection (unique angle)
- Experience with AI/technology in natural settings
- Large-scale installation capability

**Potential angles:**
1. **Shimenawa-inspired rope work** - Sacred boundaries, natural materials
2. **Sound installation** - Building on SFER IK success, responsive to forest
3. **Indigo connection** - Natural dyeing, "Japan Blue", Voronoi patterns
4. **Forest canopy engagement** - But differentiate from generic "rope in trees"

## KEY INSIGHTS FOR PROPOSAL

### 1. Emphasize "Encounters with the Unknown"

KAIR's philosophy centers on what happens when artists meet Kamiyama. The proposal should show openness to being transformed by the place, not just imposing a pre-formed vision.

### 2. Connect to Specific Kamiyama Elements

Not just "forest" but:
- Akui River blue stones
- Hinoki/sugi woodland
- Shimenawa traditions at Kaiichinomiya Shrine
- Awa indigo craft heritage
- Shikoku pilgrimage route

### 3. Demonstrate Community Value

Show how the work will involve locals:
- Learning traditional techniques
- Collaborative making
- Workshops that share knowledge both ways
- Work that serves community memory

### 4. Show Resourcefulness

They explicitly say they can't match museum resources. Proposal should demonstrate:
- Experience working with limited budgets
- Ability to source local materials
- Self-reliance in fabrication
- Adaptability

### 5. Differentiate from 2025 Application

"To Weave the Sky" didn't win. Consider:
- Different concept entirely, or
- Significantly evolved approach with Kamiyama-specific elements
- Integration of sound/technology (proven success at SFER IK)
- Stronger connection to shimenawa/sacred rope traditions
